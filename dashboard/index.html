B — The Big Four — India's Most Dangerous Species

India's polyvalent Anti-Snake Venom (ASV) covers all four species in one formulation. Species identification tells hospitals what to prepare for — ventilator, dialysis, or blood products — not which antivenom to use.

Indian Spectacled Cobra

Naja naja
Neurotoxic + Cytotoxic
Identify by
Hood with spectacle marking; spreads hood when threatened; brown to black, 1.2–1.8 m
Active
Day or early evening; rice paddies, fields, termite mounds
Bite signs
Local swelling, blistering, necrosis; then drooping eyelids (ptosis), double vision, difficulty swallowing → respiratory paralysis
Onset
Neurological symptoms within 30 min – 6 hours
Hospital prep: Monitor descending paralysis; prepare ventilator; Atropine-Neostigmine challenge — cobra responds well.

Common Krait

Bungarus caeruleus
Neurotoxic (pre-synaptic)
Identify by
Shiny black/dark with white or pale cross-bands; vertebral ridge; slender, 1.0–1.5 m
Active
Nocturnal. Bites sleeping people on floor beds; enters homes at night
Bite signs
Minimal or invisible bite marks. Victim wakes with severe abdominal pain and vomiting, then paralysis. Often misdiagnosed as food poisoning, appendicitis, or stroke
Onset
Delayed — 6–12 hours; ptosis recorded up to 36 hours after admission
Hospital prep: Pre-synaptic venom — does NOT respond to Atropine-Neostigmine; prepare mechanical ventilation (may be needed for days).

Russell's Viper

Daboia russelii
Hemotoxic + Nephrotoxic
Identify by
Stout body; three rows of dark reddish-brown oval spots on tan/brown background; loud hiss; 1.0–1.5 m
Active
Day and early darkness; farmlands, grasslands — very common in agriculture
Bite signs
Intense pain, severe swelling, bleeding from gums/bite site, blood in urine; clotting failure; kidney injury; shock. In Tamil Nadu and some Karnataka regions, also causes paralysis
Onset
Coagulopathy within 30 minutes; kidney failure over hours to days
Hospital prep: Run 20-min Whole Blood Clotting Test (20WBCT) immediately; prepare dialysis; blood products / FFP; watch for systemic capillary leak syndrome.

Saw-Scaled Viper

Echis carinatus
Hemotoxic (most common biter by volume)
Identify by
Small (0.4–0.8 m); rough keeled scales produce rasping sound when coiled; brown/grey with pale wavy pattern; dry, sandy, rocky terrain
Active
Nocturnal and diurnal; highly irritable; strikes rapidly and repeatedly
Bite signs
Local pain and swelling; bleeding from gums, nose, bite site; severe coagulopathy; unlike Russell's, rarely causes acute kidney injury
Onset
Coagulation failure within 1–3 hours
Hospital prep: Run 20WBCT; prepare blood products/FFP; dose is 6 vials ASV (lower than Russell's); monitor all bleeding sites.
C — First Aid — Immediately After a Bite

The single most important action is getting to a hospital as fast as possible. First aid buys time — it does not treat envenomation.

✅ Do These Things

  • Stay calm — 70% of bites are non-venomous; panic spreads venom faster
  • Immobilise the bitten limb like a fracture (splint with stick, spade, rolled newspaper); keep limb BELOW heart level
  • Remove jewellery, watches, tight clothing near bite
  • Transport passively — patient must NOT walk, run, or drive
  • Note the time of bite — if safe, photograph the snake without approaching it
  • Call 108 and alert the nearest hospital IMMEDIATELY
  • Nothing by mouth until at hospital

🚫 Never Do These Things

  • NEVER apply a tourniquet — causes gangrene and dramatically increases amputation rates
  • NEVER cut the wound or try to suck out venom — no evidence it works; wastes critical time
  • NEVER apply traditional remedies, black stones, herbs, or electric shock
  • NEVER attempt to kill or capture the snake — dead snakes can inject venom reflexively for hours
  • NEVER wash or interfere with the bite wound
  • NEVER give aspirin or NSAIDs — worsen haemorrhage; paracetamol only
  • NEVER let the victim walk or drive — physical exertion spreads venom through the lymphatic system
D — How Treatment Works at Hospital
ASV is the only proven treatment. It contains antibodies made by hyperimmunising horses with Big Four venom.
Polyvalent ASV covers all Big Four in one formulation — same product regardless of species.
Species info changes how the hospital monitors the patient (ventilator vs dialysis), not what is injected.
20WBCT test: 2 ml blood in a clean glass tube; if it doesn't clot in 20 min → viper envenomation confirmed → ASV immediately.
ASV is given IV only — never intramuscular, never at the bite site. Children receive the same dose as adults.
E — Myths That Kill
Myth: A tourniquet stops venom spreading
Fact: Tourniquets trap venom, destroy tissue, and cause gangrene. Immobilise the limb — do not strangle it.
Myth: Cut and suck works
Fact: Venom enters the lymphatic system within seconds — it cannot be sucked out. Cutting introduces infection and wastes time.
Myth: No pain / no bite mark = no venom
Fact: Krait bites are painless and often leave no visible mark. Symptoms appear 6–12 hours later. All suspected bites need hospital observation.
Myth: Traditional healers can treat it
Fact: No traditional remedy has proven benefit. Every hour without ASV increases the risk of death or permanent disability.
F — Prevention
Gumboots & thick trousers when working in fields, especially during monsoon (July–October = 76% of India's snakebite burden).
Sleep on raised beds with mosquito nets — Krait is nocturnal and enters homes at night.
Use a torch at night and make noise while walking; never reach into dark places without looking.
Never handle or chase a snake — even a dead one can bite reflexively for hours.
